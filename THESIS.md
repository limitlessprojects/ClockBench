# The 10-year thesis (pinned)

**We believe aging measurement is repeating the history of every clinical
biomarker: dozens of competing measures, a trust vacuum, and no referee — and
that the field's bottleneck is not another clock but the infrastructure that
decides which clocks deserve to be believed.**

Within ten years, epigenetic age becomes a routine, regulated measurement
layer in clinical trials and consumer health. The entities that own the
verification layer — the reference datasets, the parity tests, the calibration
standards — become to longevity what CLSI is to laboratory medicine and what
MLPerf is to hardware. ClockBench is built to be that layer: neutral, living,
versioned, and honest about what clocks cannot yet say.

We will know we were right when clock developers cite the spec the way
hardware vendors cite MLPerf, when trial sponsors reference the parity reports
the way they reference an analytical validation, and when the validation
business funding this work has written real checks into the companies it
standardized.

**What we are NOT claiming:** that any clock measures "true aging"; that
epigenetic age currently supports individual-level clinical decisions (the
evidence says it does not — Apsley et al. 2025); that any intervention has
been proven to rejuvenate anyone. Nobody can promise immortality, and anyone
who does is selling something. What this builds is: credibility, access,
infrastructure the field depends on, and a front-row seat — plus equity — in
the healthspan economy it helps keep honest.
